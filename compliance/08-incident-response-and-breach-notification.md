# Incident Response and Breach Notification Procedure

Version 1.0 | Owner: [OWNER NAME] | Effective: [DATE]

## 1. Purpose
This procedure says what to do when client information is lost, exposed, sent to the wrong person, or otherwise compromised, and how affected people and authorities are told.

## 2. What counts as an incident
- Lost or stolen device or paper file holding client information.
- A document or recording sent to the wrong person.
- Unauthorized access to a storage folder, email, or account.
- A phishing message that a person clicked or whose credentials were given away.
- A linguist or staff member breaking the confidentiality agreement.

## 3. Immediate steps (first 24 hours)
1. Report to the Owner immediately, by phone or email. Do not wait to confirm the facts.
2. Stop further exposure: revoke access, recall the email if possible, lock the account, or secure the device.
3. Preserve evidence: do not delete messages or logs. Note the times and who was involved.
4. Begin a written incident record with the date, time, discovery, what happened, data involved, and people affected.

## 4. Assessment (within 72 hours)
The Owner decides:
- what information was involved and whether it is sensitive (for example, Social Security numbers, bank details, health information);
- how many people are affected and where they live;
- whether the risk of harm is low or significant;
- whether the information was encrypted and the key was not compromised;
- whether protected health information is involved, which triggers the Business Associate Agreement duties.

## 5. Notification
- Clients who are covered entities: notify under the Business Associate Agreement, without unreasonable delay and within the time stated in the agreement.
- Affected individuals: notify without unreasonable delay where the incident creates a risk of harm, under Virginia law and any applicable federal rules.
- Virginia breach law: where personal information is accessed without authorization and misuse is reasonably likely, the Company notifies the Office of the Attorney General of Virginia and affected residents as the law requires. Confirm current requirements with counsel.
- Other authorities: as required by law, including federal agencies for health information where applicable.

Notices state what happened, what information was involved, what the company has done, what the person can do, and how to get more information.

## 6. Recovery
- Reset affected passwords, rotate keys, and review access.
- Securely delete copies that should no longer exist.
- Update the risk assessment and training.

## 7. Review
After every incident, the Owner completes a short written review: root cause, corrective action, owner, due date, and whether the policies need changing. Reviews are kept for three years.
